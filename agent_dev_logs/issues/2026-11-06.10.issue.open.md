Part of ufal/atrium-page-classification#58 (overview: ufal/atrium-project#83). A decision model reads a line with its context and a fixed list of categories, and returns a probability for each category. It writes no text. Here it re-judges only the lines that the rule cascade is least sure of. Its answers go to a separate table, so the categories frozen for the pilot stay as they are.

## Scope

1. **Selector** `tools/decide_lines.py`: offline, deterministic, using existing signals only. It tags five kinds of uncertain line:
   - S1: lines of at most 3 tokens, with no Czech diacritics, read `Clear` or `Noisy`;
   - S2: a 9a–9f red flag fired, but there is no conviction;
   - S3: the raw score is within ±0.05 of 0.55, or the valid-word ratio is within ±0.05 of 0.85;
   - S4: a line confidently identified as neither Czech nor Slovak, read `Noisy`;
   - S5: a document pass changed the line.
2. **Question set** `decisions/line_category.v1.json`:
   - a choice over `Clear`, `Noisy`, `Trash` and `Non-text`, worded from the vocabulary's definitions;
   - optionally, a yes/no question, "Could a reader restore the words?", for the `Noisy`/`Trash` policy.
3. **Shadow run:** one sidecar row per selected line, in `DOC_LINE_DECIDE/<doc>.csv`. A row holds the tag, the rule's category, the model's choice and probabilities, the confidence, the lane, the status and the latency. `categ`, the record and `PAGE_ALTO/` are not touched.
4. **Evaluation** on the 2,067-line gold set, split by document into dev and test, with the existing verdict of `tools/recategorize_from_csv.py --gold-column gold_categ`. The #30 annotations are a second test set.
5. **Models:**
   - Clef first, on Pavel's internal endpoint;
   - then Clef-Flash;
   - Strands Decider 2B, which reads text and runs on a CPU, as the cost target.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `decision_client.py` is copied from page-classification and pinned in `tests/test_vendored_decision_parity.py`.
- [ ] The size of each tag's population is reported on the gold set and over `DOC_LINE_CATEG_1210`.
- [ ] The question set is generated from the vocabulary, and its digest is recorded in the paradata.
- [ ] Each run records where the lines were sent (the internal endpoint or the cluster), as agreed on ufal/atrium-project#83.
- [ ] Each model is scored per tag on the gold test half:
  - fixed and broken lines;
  - the confusion matrix and `Trash` recall;
  - calibration (Brier, ECE);
  - invalid answers and timeouts.
- [ ] The wording, the packing (one line or one page per request) and the lane thresholds are tuned on dev only, and frozen before test.
- [ ] The cost of re-scoring both collections is estimated from the measured throughput.
- [ ] A recommendation for each tag (stop, keep, or a follow-up) is posted here.

For Dana two questions on how the decision model will be asked about lines, from Opus.

For each uncertain line (the S1–S5 groups in the description), the model will receive the line, its uncorrected OCR text, the two lines before and after it, and the detected language with its score. It is asked to choose one of `Clear`, `Noisy`, `Trash` or `Non-text`, and it returns a probability for each of the four, adding up to 1. It writes no text, so its answer is always one of the four, though it can be the wrong one. Nothing it answers changes a line or its stored category: the answers go to a separate table and are scored against the 2,067 hand-labelled gold lines.

Apart from the four names, the model is told only one short description per category. Today these would come from the shared vocabulary:

- `Clear`: "Structurally sound text with low perplexity."
- `Noisy`: "Partially degraded text (e.g., isolated strange symbols, mid-word uppercase)."
- `Trash`: "Severe OCR corruption, high symbol density, gibberish, or failed language ID."
- `Non-text`: "Lines that are too short, lack letters, or are purely numbers/symbols."

"Low perplexity" and "failed language ID" describe our measurements, not what a reader sees on the line.

1. Could you write one sentence per category, as you would explain it to a new annotator? Those sentences would replace the four descriptions above.
2. Lines like `1 fraament okraie` (530 in the collection) are stored as `Trash`, although a reader can tell what they say. Should the archive count them as `Noisy` or as `Trash`? Your answer sets their correct label, and the meaning of a second, yes/no question: "Could a reader restore the words of this line?"

P.S.

Since 6 November, the model is also asked about every line, not only S1–S5:

- a quality score from 0 (no recognisable words) to 4 (correct text);
- ten yes/no degradation factors that restate the rule detectors in a reader's words, such as stray symbols, gibberish, misread letters, split or merged words and mirrored text;
- whether the line is in another language.

The category question stays on the S1–S5 lines.

`tools/decide_lines.py --decide` writes the answers to `DOC_LINE_DECIDE.csv`, a separate file; no category or rule changes. Offline only; nothing has been run on a model yet.

#### <img src="https://avatars.githubusercontent.com/u/65828620?u=80b39d3cb2967c52e1d4af28dcefc7a1efdfdc79&v=4" width="50">[K4TEL](https://github.com/K4TEL) commented at [2026-11-08 17:30](https://github.com/ufal/atrium-ocr-postprocess/issues/10#issuecomment-6724705718):

**Decision models, briefly.** A decision model is a language model that chooses instead of writing. It gets an input (a page image, a text, or both) and questions whose answers are fixed in advance: yes/no, one option from a list, or a point on a scale. It returns a probability for every allowed answer, so it never answers off the list, though it can choose wrongly. It is not trained on our data: a short task description, the question and one line per answer are all it is told. One pass answers dozens of questions, so it is cheap, and the probabilities mark the cases it is unsure of, which a person or the existing tool can then decide.

Here it reads text only. Two question sets:

| Set | Asked about | Answer | Checked against |
|---|---|---|---|
| Category | the S1–S5 lines | 1 of the 4 categories | the 2,067 gold lines |
| Quality | every line | a 0–4 score; 10 yes/no signs of damage; yes/no "another language" | the gold lines (score); the rule checks (signs) |

Measured, beside what the description lists for the category:
- **Quality score:** whether it separates the gold `Clear`, `Noisy` and `Trash` lines better than the rules' own score does.
- **Signs of damage:** each one restates a rule check in a reader's words, so comparing the two shows where the model and the rules read a line differently. This is agreement, not accuracy: no line is labelled for the signs.
